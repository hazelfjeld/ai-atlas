Subgroup evaluation

Measuring performance separately for meaningful subsets of data.

Expanded guide · editorial review 2026-11-02. Original definitions, examples, notes and citations are preserved below. New examples are illustrative, not benchmark results.

Prerequisites

  • Machine learning
  • Distribution shift
  • Worst-group accuracy
  • Calibration
  • Equal opportunity (fairness)

Why aggregate results can be misleading

Subgroup evaluation measures model performance separately for meaningful slices of a target population or environment instead of relying only on aggregate accuracy. Hidden stratification can leave rare, clinically or operationally important categories with poor outcomes even when average scores look strong. A subgroup comparison is a diagnostic about sampled conditions, not automatically evidence of discrimination, an unaligned objective or a real-world incident.

Define groups and the population before the audit

Specify the use case, prediction unit, desired target population, labels, decision thresholds and authorized group metadata in a written protocol. Select slices that reflect known risks, deployment sites, time periods, language varieties, acquisition devices or legally and ethically appropriate demographic attributes. Document unknown or missing group labels, overlap and data provenance. Do not infer sensitive attributes from unrelated user data just to construct a dashboard.

Work a synthetic example that exposes the gap

Imagine 100 completely synthetic test cases: group A contains 90 and is correct on 81, so accuracy is 90%; group B contains 10 and is correct on four, so accuracy is 40%. Pooled accuracy is (81+4)/(90+10)=85%, while the worst-group accuracy is 40% and the group accuracy gap is 50 percentage points. These numbers are teaching arithmetic, not results from an external model. With only ten cases in group B, uncertainty is substantial and should be communicated.

Measure task-relevant errors and denominators

Choose measures that match the task: sensitivity and false-negative rate for screening, false-positive rate for interventions, calibration for probabilities, and task success plus unauthorized-action rate for agents. Show TP, FP, TN and FN counts and denominators for every slice; prevalence changes can make precision differ even at similar sensitivity. Compare with a frozen threshold and report both aggregate and worst-group metrics, not a single favorable comparison.

Quantify uncertainty for small and correlated groups

Small groups have noisy estimates: a 40% result from four successes in ten trials should not be reported as a precise true population rate. Use appropriate binomial intervals or hierarchical methods when justified, avoid normal approximations with very small counts, and resample by independent people, sessions or sites if cases share an origin. Flag suppressed estimates and wide intervals explicitly rather than ranking noisy subgroups as if their ordering were certain.

Check intersectional slices and multiple comparisons

A model might perform well for each broad language or region group while failing their intersection or an unmeasured rare subtype. Evaluate predeclared intersections where authorized and adequately powered, with protections for small cells and disclosure risk. Exploratory slicing across hundreds of groups can produce extreme chance findings; distinguish exploratory hypotheses from confirmatory results and use fresh holdout evidence before asserting persistent disparities.

Stress-test transfer across time and environment

Sagawa and colleagues investigate worst-group robustness for defined groups; the WILDS benchmark tests distribution shifts across real acquisition settings. A strong worst-group score on one benchmark does not guarantee reliability at a new hospital, across changing sensors, or for unobserved groups. Retain time and site splits, document the group taxonomy and assess unlabeled deployment coverage where permissible; updating a model requires a separate untouched evaluation.

Run a controlled reproducible subgroup audit

Proposed evaluation, not a completed experiment: create or select authorized synthetic examples spanning two environments and three prespecified task-relevant slices, split by entity and time, freeze a model and thresholds, then produce per-slice confusion matrices, accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, calibration and sample counts. Calculate pooled and minimum observed subgroup metrics with uncertainty intervals across seeds. Register exploratory exceptions and rerun on a sealed test set before claiming any failure generalizes.

Choose mitigations without hiding tradeoffs

Potential responses include additional representative training data, measurement improvements, robust optimization, threshold review and workflow-specific human escalation. Group distributionally robust optimization can target worst-group risk under specific assumptions, but changing weights can also alter average accuracy or other group outcomes. Reevaluate on held-out slices after each proposed mitigation; do not use demographic thresholds or collect protected data without appropriate legal, ethical and organizational review.

Evidence limitations and publication boundary

Oakden-Rayner and colleagues document hidden stratification in medical-image research; Mitchell and colleagues propose model cards with disaggregated performance reporting; Sagawa and colleagues study group-robust optimization; Koh and colleagues introduce WILDS. These works motivate structured evaluation but do not prove any particular deployed product is unsafe. The invented arithmetic and protocol here are educational only and neither establishes intentional deception nor claims a new external incident.
