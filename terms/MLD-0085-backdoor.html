Security, privacy, and fairness · MLD-0085

Backdoor

A learned or inserted behavior activated by a trigger while remaining less visible on ordinary inputs.

Expanded guide · editorial review 2026-11-02. Original definitions, examples, notes and citations are preserved below. New examples are illustrative, not benchmark results.

Prerequisites

  • Data poisoning
  • Adversarial example
  • Adversarial evaluation
  • Adversarial robustness

What distinguishes a learned backdoor

A model backdoor is a learned or deliberately inserted condition that changes behavior when a trigger occurs, often while ordinary inputs look normal. It differs from a runtime software backdoor and from a general adversarial example: the hidden conditional behavior was already present in the model. Test-time trigger access and model supply-chain control are separate threat assumptions.

The conditional risk behind the clean score

Let A_clean denote accuracy on ordinary inputs and ASR denote attack success rate on designated triggered inputs. A model can have high A_clean and high ASR simultaneously; neither measure should be substituted for the other. Backdoor research such as BadNets illustrates this separation in controlled image-classifier settings, not proof of a live deployment compromise.

Synthetic trigger-test arithmetic

Imagine a toy classifier with 96 correct outputs among 100 clean holdout examples, giving 96% clean accuracy. On a separate set of 40 artificially triggered examples, suppose 28 switch to an attacker-chosen label: ASR = 28 / 40 = 70%. This invented example illustrates conditional failure; it does not predict a particular real model or validate an unknown trigger.

How backdoors can enter a pipeline

Threat surfaces include compromised pretraining data, attacker-controlled fine-tuning records, external model weights, outsourced training and unauthorized artifact replacement. A trigger can be visual, textual or contextual depending on the system. Record who can modify data or weights, where cryptographic artifact checks occur, and which training stages are exposed; a provenance log alone cannot guarantee semantic safety.

Trigger definitions and test coverage

An evaluation must specify the tested input distribution, trigger family, placement variability, intended target behavior and who knows the trigger. Reporting only successful triggered examples selects for the attacker's wins; report the denominator and confidence uncertainty. Include benign patterns resembling a trigger so false activation and ordinary user impact are measurable.

Detection as a fallible diagnostic

A reviewer might compare clean/triggered behavior, inspect neurons or representations, or test candidate input patterns. Neural Cleanse explored reconstructing possible triggers, while later attack variants exposed limits of particular detection assumptions. A detector can miss an adaptive backdoor or wrongly flag legitimate features; detection scores are not forensic proof of malicious intent.

Mitigation tradeoffs and revalidation

Fine-pruning studies combine pruning and fine-tuning to reduce demonstrated backdoor effects under evaluated conditions. Removing a suspicious feature may damage legitimate functionality, and fine-tuning alone need not remove a persistent trigger. Compare clean accuracy, ASR, subgroup behavior and regression cases after any intervention, and maintain rollback plus signed-artifact provenance.

Reproducible isolated defensive assessment

In a controlled local sandbox, create two toy models with declared data provenance, keep untouched clean and trigger-like holdouts, and blind the evaluator to which model was modified. Record model hashes, training budgets, test sample counts and all negative results. Use a predeclared escalation threshold and never interpret a narrow trigger probe as a comprehensive certification.

Boundary between experiments and incidents

BadNets and related backdoor papers demonstrate mechanisms in research settings; they do not establish that an arbitrary deployed assistant has a backdoor. Conversely, ordinary task performance cannot exclude dormant triggers. Document evidence type, model versions, access constraints and independent replications before describing a real-world compromise.
