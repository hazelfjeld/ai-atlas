Interpretability and mechanistic analysis · MLD-0157

Causal mediation analysis

Studying how an intervention's effect passes through specified intermediate variables or components.

Expanded guide · editorial review 2026-11-03. Original definitions, examples, notes and citations are preserved below. New examples are illustrative, not benchmark results.

Prerequisites

  • Causal intervention
  • Confounding
  • Counterfactual
  • Mechanistic interpretability

Mediation separates pathways rather than just outcomes

Causal mediation analysis asks how much of a specified exposure-to-outcome effect travels through a proposed intermediate variable M, versus pathways not mediated by M. In a language model, a mediator might be one layer, attention head or feature; in an observational study it could be a real-world intermediate. These are different domains with different assumptions about causal identification.

A three-variable causal picture

Imagine X influences a mediator M, which influences an output Y, while X may also influence Y through other routes. The total effect compares the outcome under two exposure settings. A proposed indirect effect additionally asks what would happen if M were held to the value arising from another exposure. Without clearly naming the contrast, 'the mediator explains 60%' is ambiguous.

The counterfactual definition has assumptions

Natural direct and indirect effects often invoke nested potential outcomes such as Y(x,M(x0)); identifying those from observational records generally needs strong no-unmeasured-confounding and cross-world assumptions. Randomizing an exposure alone does not automatically randomize its mediator. In a fully observed deterministic network, direct activation replacement is feasible, yet a semantic interpretation of that feature can still be wrong.

A transparent synthetic arithmetic example

Suppose a toy model's output score changes from 0.20 under the baseline to 0.80 under a modified input, a total score change of 0.60. A matched intervention on a candidate mediator yields 0.45, a change of 0.25 from baseline. Under this chosen operational contrast, 0.25/0.60 is approximately 42%; it is not an identified natural indirect-effect fraction or a statement that one neuron owns 42% of the meaning.

Mediators in transformer language models

Vig and colleagues used causal mediation ideas to investigate gender-bias measurements in pretrained Transformer language models, studying how effects vary across neurons and attention heads. Their reported patterns are conditional on particular datasets, prompts and effect definitions. It would overstate the result to claim that a universal bias location or a universally causal head was discovered.

Patch-based mediation and causal tracing

Meng and colleagues contrasted clean, corrupted and restored hidden-state runs to identify computations influencing factual predictions, then studied targeted weight edits. This is a controlled intervention-based analysis of particular transformer models and benchmark prompts. Recovering an output probability after restoration is an effect estimate for that procedure, not independent validation of the output's factual truth.

Interactions, redundancy and path dependence

Two components can be synergistic, suppressive or redundant, making individual effect magnitudes non-additive. An indirect-effect estimate may even be negative while the total effect is positive; simply dividing by the total can yield misleading shares or unstable ratios when the denominator is small. Always report the underlying effect definitions, both signs, interaction tests and confidence intervals.

Isolation and sensitivity checks

Predefine candidate mediators, keep base/source examples matched, try mediator controls with similar activation scale, and reserve held-out prompts for confirmation. For real-world observational mediation, consider exposure-mediator and mediator-outcome confounding and sensitivity to unmeasured causes. For networks, test distribution shift from patching and alternative alignment choices; neither framework is assumption-free.

What a mediation result can establish

A carefully designed study can support that altering a specified intermediary changes a specified measured outcome under a defined intervention. It cannot by itself establish exclusive storage, human-level understanding, unique causal mechanism, independent fairness or safety guarantees, or field-wide prevalence. Keep synthetic examples, controlled research findings and real deployment incidents distinctly labeled.
