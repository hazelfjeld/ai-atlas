Security, privacy, and fairness · MLD-0268

Data poisoning

Adversarial manipulation of training or adaptation data to influence learned behavior.

Expanded guide · editorial review 2026-11-02. Original definitions, examples, notes and citations are preserved below. New examples are illustrative, not benchmark results.

Prerequisites

  • Adversarial evaluation
  • Adversarial robustness
  • Backdoor
  • Data leakage

Poisoning changes what the learner sees

Data poisoning alters training, fine-tuning, preference, retrieval-index or other adaptation examples before they affect a learned system. The attack targets the data-to-model trust boundary, unlike test-time evasion, which changes a prediction input after training. A suspicious example is not automatically malicious; verify provenance and intent.

Attack goals and control boundaries

An attacker may try to degrade general accuracy, bias a selected class, plant a trigger-linked backdoor or steer a model toward a target answer. Define whether the attacker can insert, replace, relabel or prioritize records, how many they control, and whether the defender retrains from scratch or merely adapts a frozen model. Success claims without these conditions are not portable.

A simple contamination-rate calculation

In an invented toy dataset with 9,950 trusted records and 50 maliciously edited records, the suspicious fraction is 50 / 10,000 = 0.5%. That number describes input control, not the percentage of predictions the attacker can change. One targeted failure might occur with a small fraction; another task may resist far larger contamination. Neither inference follows from the count alone.

Mechanism: changing a training objective

Ordinary empirical risk takes the mean loss over the provided training examples. A poisoner changes some of those examples, potentially changing gradient updates, labels, correlations or the optimizer's solution. The causal question is how an otherwise identical training run differs when only the suspected records change; a large before-after difference without matched controls can be confounded.

Web-scale provenance and mutable sources

Dataset indexes may store URLs rather than immutable content bytes, while public documents may be edited between selection and ingestion. Carlini and colleagues analyzed split-view and snapshot-timing threats in a controlled research setting. For a defender, snapshot provenance, cryptographic content hashes, timestamps and reproducible acquisition can narrow this ambiguity; a URL alone is not content integrity.

Backdoor versus indiscriminate degradation

Availability-style poisoning increases errors broadly, whereas targeted poisoning attempts a selected response or class and can leave overall accuracy nearly unchanged. A backdoor additionally links an unusual condition to the target behavior. Evaluate clean accuracy, targeted attack success and false activation under matched test conditions; a clean aggregate score does not establish absence of a targeted problem.

Defensive data pipeline and limits

Restrict write access, track origin and license, version datasets, hash artifacts, separate review from training, screen anomalous label-content pairs and investigate high-influence sources. Deduplication and outlier filters help quality control but do not prove resistance: subtle malicious records may resemble ordinary examples, and aggressive filtering can discard legitimate minority data.

Controlled audit with independent holdouts

For an authorized isolated experiment, freeze a clean reference set and independent held-out labels; compare a baseline training run with a run containing transparently labeled synthetic perturbations. Fix seeds and budgets or report variation, inspect subgroup outcomes and targeted failure rates, and retain a manifest of every changed training record. Do not seed public corpora or operational datasets during a demonstration.

What conclusions can actually be drawn

A measured attack success rate applies only to a specified model, dataset, attack budget, threat model and evaluation suite. Simulation and published vulnerability demonstrations are not proof that a particular commercial model was compromised. A passing scanner or unchanged aggregate accuracy is not proof of safety; document residual risks, retraining controls and unresolved evidence.
