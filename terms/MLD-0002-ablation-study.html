Statistics and experimental design · MLD-0002

Ablation study

An experiment removing or changing components to test their contribution.

Expanded guide · editorial review 2026-11-02. Original definitions, examples, notes and citations are preserved below. New examples are illustrative, not benchmark results.

Prerequisites

  • Baseline
  • Causal intervention
  • Internal validity
  • Overfitting

What an ablation can actually test

An ablation compares a full system with a deliberately modified version, usually removing or disabling one component while holding the evaluation task fixed. The observed difference estimates the contribution of that intervention under tested conditions; it is not automatically the component's intrinsic importance in every possible system.

State the estimand before touching the model

Write a hypothesis such as: removing retrieval reduces held-out answer accuracy under the same retrieval corpus, test split, prompt budget and evaluator. Prespecify the direction, primary metric, minimum meaningful difference and experimental unit. Selecting the most impressive result after examining many alternatives can distort the conclusion.

Retraining versus inference-time removal

Removing a module during inference probes how a trained network currently depends on it. Retraining a smaller architecture without that module probes what a different network can learn. Other weights may compensate in retraining; neither approach alone establishes that a component is necessary in every configuration.

Worked miniature comparison

Imagine a full classifier scores 0.81 macro-F1 and an otherwise matched variant without an auxiliary loss scores 0.76 on a sealed test set: the observed difference is 0.05 F1 points. These numbers are invented teaching values, not published results. Repeat across paired random seeds and report uncertainty before claiming that the auxiliary loss helped.

Interactions require crossed experiments

If two components A and B interact, removing each alone cannot establish their joint contribution. Train four configurations: neither, A alone, B alone and both; compare the incremental effect of A with and without B. A component may appear redundant when another substitutes for it but useful when both are absent.

Fair controls for compute and configuration

Record parameter counts, training tokens, GPU budget, hyperparameter search effort, data selection, seeds, stop criteria and checkpoint-selection rules. A variant given less compute may lose for reasons unrelated to the missing mechanism. Compare fixed-budget performance and matched-quality resource use when the distinction matters.

Statistical and split hygiene

Use identical held-out examples and paired random seeds when feasible, report variation across tasks, and calculate uncertainty intervals. Do not tune the model on the final test set; fit preprocessing only inside training folds. One favorable seed or repeated leaderboard feedback is weak evidence for a robust causal effect.

Ablations for AI-safety research

In a controlled specification-gaming study, vary the presence of a reward-proxy shortcut while keeping the task distribution and evaluation protocol fixed. Measure proxy score and independent ground-truth task success separately, add a placebo change and audit high-scoring outlier trajectories. This is a proposed experiment, not evidence of intentional deception in deployment.

Limits, misreadings and reproducibility

Ablating a module can simultaneously alter input distributions, optimization, training dynamics and model capacity. A performance drop may reflect general brittleness rather than a unique concept; unchanged performance may reflect redundancy or an insensitive metric. Share configurations, source code, seeds, statistical uncertainty and negative results so alternative explanations can be checked.
