Adversarial robustness

Resistance to adversarially chosen inputs or perturbations within a specified threat model.

Expanded guide · editorial review 2026-11-02. Original definitions, examples, notes and citations are preserved below. New examples are illustrative, not benchmark results.

Prerequisites

  • Adversarial example
  • Adversarial evaluation

Robustness depends on a threat model

Adversarial robustness means maintaining a specified property despite maliciously selected inputs inside a defined attack set. A classifier may be robust to bounded image perturbations yet fail under camera changes, distribution shift, data poisoning or prompt injection. Always name the property and attack set.

Robust risk as a minimax objective

Ordinary empirical risk minimizes mean loss L(f(x),y). Robust training often minimizes E[ max over delta in S of L(f(x+delta),y) ]. The inner maximum models an adversary's allowed perturbations; the outer minimum adjusts model parameters. Practical approximations do not certify the true maximum.

Synthetic robust-accuracy calculation

Suppose an invented frozen test set contains 100 labeled samples. A classifier predicts 92 correctly before attack, but only 63 correctly after an allowed bounded attack is applied to each sample. Its clean accuracy is 92% and empirical attacked accuracy 63% for that specific suite, not a universal guarantee.

Threat-model choices that change the result

Specify norm and epsilon, input scale, clipping and preprocessing, test-time access, target selection, query budget and allowed changes to content. For image bytes in [0,255], epsilon = 8 differs from epsilon = 8/255 for normalized inputs; an undocumented scaling mismatch can invalidate comparisons.

Attack strength and gradient masking

Weak or mismatched attacks can overstate defensive performance. Evaluate with well-configured multi-step and diverse attacks, check whether attack success rises with effort, and test differentiability or randomization assumptions. Gradient masking is a warning sign, not proof that a defense has stopped evasion.

Empirical robustness versus certification

Empirical testing demonstrates survival against attacks actually tried; certified robustness proves a bounded property under explicit formal assumptions and computational methods. Neither guarantees reliability beyond the certified region, model version, preprocessing pipeline, class set or operational data regime.

The clean–robust tradeoff

Adversarial training can increase resistance within the selected perturbation model but may require additional computation or change ordinary test performance. Quantify both with matched architecture, training budget and seeds; a better score on one epsilon or dataset cannot establish across-the-board superiority.

Use conditions in safety-critical workflows

Robust evaluation helps when an attacker can manipulate model inputs or when bounded perturbation tolerance is a concrete requirement. Combine it with shift monitoring, provenance checks, access controls and out-of-distribution testing. Do not label a model secure against tool misuse or prompt injection based on an image-attack benchmark.

Auditable experiment and limitations

Pre-register threat model and thresholds, freeze an independent test split, compare a standard baseline with a robust alternative, and test with adaptive white-box and complementary black-box methods. Publish clean/attacked metrics, attack configurations, failure modes, confidence intervals and any untested adversary capabilities.
