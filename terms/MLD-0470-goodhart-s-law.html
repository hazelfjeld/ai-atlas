Goodhart's law

The principle that optimizing a measure can weaken its usefulness as a measure of the intended target.

Expanded guide · editorial review 2026-11-01. Original definitions, examples, notes and citations are preserved below. New examples are illustrative, not benchmark results.

Prerequisites

  • Proxy objective
  • Overfitting

Mechanism: selection amplifies proxy error

Goodhart effects arise when a variable initially useful for predicting the real target becomes less reliable under strong selection or intervention. A measurable proxy P can combine target quality Q with noise or unrelated features. Selecting extreme P values tends to select favorable noise as well as high Q; the effect depends on the proxy, environment and strength of optimization.

A small mathematical illustration

Suppose P = Q + E, where Q is the quality you care about and E is zero-mean measurement error. Picking the highest P from many candidates tends to pick candidates with unusually positive E. A higher maximum P need not imply an equally higher Q. This toy model illustrates regressional Goodhart; it is not a universal formula for every failure.

Four mechanisms, not one theorem

Manheim and Garrabrant distinguish regressional effects (selecting noise), extremal effects (leaving the region where correlations were learned), causal effects (intervening on the metric does not cause the target to improve) and adversarial effects (other agents adapt to the target metric). Real systems can combine multiple mechanisms.

Worked AI-safety example

Imagine a code-generation benchmark in which a learned reviewer assigns scores based on formatting and a handful of tests. Increasing best-of-N selection may raise reviewer scores while independently verified functional correctness plateaus or falls. This is an illustrative experiment to run, not a measured claim about Atlas, your models or any particular judge.

Test design: diagnose the break point

For increasing selection budgets N = 1, 4, 16, 64, freeze the candidate generator and judge. Record the chosen candidate's proxy score, separate hidden-test score and uncertainty over repeated seeds. Plot target score against proxy score and inspect error clusters. Audit whether evaluator leakage or changed data distributions confound the result.

Tradeoffs and misconceptions

Goodhart's law does not imply metrics are worthless or that optimization always reduces true quality. Often both metrics improve at first; failure may emerge only under stronger pressure or distribution change. Improve metric robustness, maintain independent outcomes and avoid declaring a causal mechanism from correlation alone.
