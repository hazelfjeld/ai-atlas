Evaluation metrics and uncertainty · MLD-0389

Expected calibration error

A binned summary of differences between predicted confidence and empirical correctness or frequency.

Expanded guide · editorial review 2026-11-02. Original definitions, examples, notes and citations are preserved below. New examples are illustrative, not benchmark results.

Prerequisites

  • Machine learning
  • Calibration
  • Reliability diagram
  • Brier score
  • Distribution shift

Define the event before computing the metric

Expected calibration error (ECE) summarizes disagreement between predicted confidence and empirical correctness after partitioning predictions into confidence bins. In common top-label ECE, confidence is the predicted probability of the highest-scoring class and correctness is whether that chosen class matches the label. Other calibration tasks, including classwise and multilabel calibration, require different event definitions; the same ECE number should not be compared across incompatible definitions.

Write down the estimator and its assumptions

For disjoint confidence bins B_1,...,B_K covering n predictions, top-label ECE = sum_k (|B_k|/n) * abs(acc(B_k) - conf(B_k)), where acc is the observed fraction correct and conf is the mean reported confidence. Fix the bin edges, tie conventions, target population and classification interface before viewing outcomes. This finite-sample estimator is not an unbiased, distribution-free certificate of the model's true calibration error.

Calculate a completely synthetic ten-case ECE

Invent ten labeled predictions: four have confidence 0.75 with two correct, while six have confidence 0.90 with five correct. Suppose these are separate, predeclared confidence bins. The first accuracy is 2/4 = 0.50 and its absolute gap is 0.25. The second accuracy is 5/6 = 0.8333 and its gap is about 0.0667. ECE = (4/10)*0.25 + (6/10)*(0.90-5/6) = 0.10+0.04 = 0.14, or 14 percentage points. These numbers are illustrative, not measured benchmark results.

Inspect bin construction rather than a score alone

Equal-width bins can contain very different numbers of observations; equal-mass bins change widths according to the sampled confidences. The estimated score can move substantially when K, interval boundaries or empty-bin handling changes. Always report bin sizes and reliability diagrams, repeat with prespecified alternative binning schemes, and examine variance by resampling entire independent evaluation units rather than assuming each correlated prediction is independent.

Contrast ECE with proper scoring rules

A low ECE can coexist with low classification accuracy or poor discrimination; an overconfident rare error can also disappear in a broad bin's average. Log loss and the Brier score reward honest predictive distributions according to specified scoring rules, but neither is interchangeable with ECE. Report accuracy, base rates, Brier score or log loss, classwise calibration and confidence intervals together so that optimizing one convenient summary does not conceal failures elsewhere.

Check classes, subgroups and distribution shift

Pooled ECE weights common groups heavily; a small high-risk subgroup can remain miscalibrated even when the pooled estimate is low. If appropriately collected and authorized, evaluate separately by prespecified subgroup, class, time period and deployment domain, with sample counts and uncertainty shown. Calibration learned on one distribution can deteriorate under covariate or label shift. Do not infer reliable per-person confidence or safety from a satisfactory global score.

Perform a bounded, reproducible audit

Proposed experiment, not an executed study: freeze a classifier and a split with independent entities, record top-label confidence and correctness on untouched test items, and pre-register 5-bin and 10-bin ECE plus Brier score, accuracy and classwise figures. Compute bin counts, calibration gaps, optional bootstrap intervals clustered by entity, and compare fresh time-ordered test slices. If trying temperature scaling, fit it only on a distinct calibration split, never on the final test labels.

Guard against evaluation and optimization games

Choosing bin boundaries after inspecting failures, filtering low-confidence cases without disclosure, reusing test labels for temperature tuning, or presenting only the best ECE estimator can produce misleading conclusions. Pair the result with a sealed holdout, a declared denominator and a documented missing-label rate. Goodhart effects can arise if model selection optimizes a single coarse binning statistic while deployment-relevant error types and distribution shifts remain unchecked.

Explain limitations and evidence provenance

Guo and colleagues popularized practical neural-network calibration diagnostics and temperature scaling; Nixon and colleagues compared the sensitivity of calibration measures; Kumar and colleagues studied limits of calibration error estimation. Their evidence concerns classifier calibration methods and statistical estimation, not proof of aligned objectives or a newly observed AI safety incident. A measured ECE is a sample-dependent diagnostic, not a guarantee of safe actions, subgroup parity or future performance.
