Guided demo · about 3 minutes

From a question
to the evidence.

A research workspace that brings AI safety papers, evidence-labeled reports, practical playbooks, and a growing technology atlas into one place.

A simple opening

“I'm building Atlas as an AI learning and research hub. The goal is to move from a concept or concern to its sources, limitations, and a practical next step—not just collect headlines.”

01 / ASK A RESEARCH QUESTION

Start with reward hacking.

Search the paper library. Open a record and show its summary, limitations, review label, and link to the original paper.

Point to make: the explanation and its evidence stay together.

Open the research search →
02 / INSPECT A SAFETY CLAIM

Keep the context attached.

Open the safety observatory. Use the report-type filter, inspect a record's environment and timeline, and explain whether it describes a real-world report or a controlled evaluation.

Point to make: a concerning demonstration is not automatically a confirmed deployment incident.

Explore the safety reports →
03 / MAKE IT PRACTICAL

Move from reading to a plan.

Browse the engineering playbooks. Choose one related to the conversation and show its suggested approach, first step, validation plan, and tradeoffs.

Point to make: the hub is intended to support experiments and decisions, not just definitions.

Open the problem playbooks →
04 / COMPARE THE OPTIONS

Explore tools and tradeoffs.

Open the technology atlas, filter the published profiles and compare Amazon Bedrock with Amazon SageMaker AI. The comparison is editorial, not an independent benchmark.

Point to make: technology choices are contextual rather than a universal leaderboard; only the published subset is shown.

Compare published technologies →
05 / SHOW WHAT IS REAL

Finish with an honest inventory.

Open publication progress. It separates what is published from what is merely prepared. Use the counts in this release instead of quoting an old screenshot.

Point to make: the catalog is incomplete, evidence labels have limits, and a scheduled development task is not a live research feed.

Show publication progress →

A useful question for your professors

“Which evidence standard, learning feature, or evaluation would make this genuinely useful for your students or research?”

Before presenting: open each step once on the device you will use. Keep a known-good commit-pinned GitHack link as a fallback. This guide is printable and needs no JavaScript; the catalog pages do require JavaScript and a network connection.
